Pharma document translation

Reading time: 4 min

Pharmaceutical and life-science companies handle enormous volumes of regulated documents: clinical study reports, protocols, regulatory submissions, product information and correspondence with health authorities across Europe and beyond. Accuracy is non-negotiable — a mistranslated dosage or adverse-event term has regulatory consequences.

Why pharma translation is specialized

Three constraints define pharma document translation. Terminology is controlled: MedDRA, ATC codes and EMA/FDA-standard phrasing must be used consistently — "adverse event" is not interchangeable with "side effect". Format matters: SmPCs, IMPDs and CTD modules follow strict templates where section order and boilerplate are mandated. And volume is relentless: a single marketing authorisation generates hundreds of pages needing translation into every EU language.

DataCompiler's human-review step targets exactly the passages where terminology matters — flagged uncertain text is verified before delivery. For regulated submissions, this reviewed translation serves as the working draft that specialist medical translators then certify.

Document types commonly translated

  • Clinical study reports and protocols
  • Product information: SmPCs, PILs, labels
  • Regulatory correspondence with EMA, national agencies, IRBs
  • Pharmacovigilance documents and safety reports
  • Training materials and SOPs

Languages covered

DataCompiler translates between English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Polish, Turkish and Chinese (Simplified) — the core set for EU regulatory work. Handwritten annotations on scanned documents, common in investigator files, are supported with human review.

Regulatory caveat

For official submissions to EMA or national agencies, check whether certified translation by an authorised specialist is mandated. DataCompiler's output is a high-quality reviewed translation ideal for internal review, due diligence and draft preparation.
