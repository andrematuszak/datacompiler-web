Pharma document translation

Reading time: 5 min

Pharmaceutical and life-science companies handle enormous volumes of regulated documents: clinical study reports, protocols, regulatory submissions, product information and correspondence with health authorities across Europe and beyond. A single marketing authorisation can generate hundreds of pages that need translation into every EU language — and accuracy is non-negotiable, because a mistranslated dosage or adverse-event term has regulatory consequences.

Why pharma translation is specialized

Three constraints define pharmaceutical document translation. Terminology is controlled: MedDRA, ATC codes and EMA/FDA-standard phrasing must be used consistently — "adverse event" is not interchangeable with "side effect", and each has a defined term in every language. Format is mandated: SmPCs, IMPDs and CTD modules follow strict templates where section order, headings and boilerplate are prescribed. Volume is relentless: regulatory work arrives in batches that need fast turnaround without sacrificing review quality.

This is exactly where DataCompiler's human-review step matters: passages the engine flags as uncertain — terminology, numbers, codes — are verified by a reviewer before delivery. For regulated submissions, this reviewed translation serves as the working draft that specialist medical translators then certify.

Document types commonly translated

  • Clinical documents — study reports, protocols, investigator brochures, informed consent forms
  • Product information — SmPCs (Summary of Product Characteristics), PILs (Patient Information Leaflets), labels and packaging texts
  • Regulatory correspondence — letters to EMA, national agencies, IRBs and ethics committees
  • Pharmacovigilance — safety reports, periodic safety update reports (PSURs), risk-management documents
  • Internal documentation — SOPs, training materials, quality-system documents
