Chinese document translation

Reading time: 4 min

China is one of the world's largest trading partners, and Chinese document translation is one of the most requested language services: contracts with Chinese suppliers and partners, certificates of origin, business licenses, academic credentials and official paperwork all move between Chinese and European languages daily.

Which Chinese? Simplified vs Traditional

This is the first question for any Chinese translation job. Simplified Chinese is the standard in mainland China and Singapore; Traditional Chinese is used in Taiwan, Hong Kong and Macau. DataCompiler works in Simplified Chinese — if your document is headed to Taiwan or Hong Kong, tell us before ordering, since the script choice affects every character.

Translating documents into Chinese

European and American companies translating into Chinese typically handle contracts, technical documentation, product certifications and marketing materials. Chinese has no spaces between words and no alphabet, so text expansion works differently than with European languages — layout re-typesetting matters more than raw word count.

Translating Chinese documents out

The reverse direction is just as common: Chinese business licenses, contracts, certificates and government documents need English or European-language versions for customs, banks, regulators and partners. Chinese documents often carry seals (chops), stamps and handwritten annotations — elements that benefit from the human-review step.

Read more: Translate Chinese documents.

Why scans and handwriting matter

Many Chinese documents exist only as scans, photos or signed paper originals. Generic translation tools cannot read them — they need OCR first. DataCompiler transcribes the source text before translating, so a photographed contract or a handwritten letter works the same as a native PDF. Uncertain passages are flagged for human review rather than silently guessed.
