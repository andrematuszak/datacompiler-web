Legal document translation

Reading time: 4 min

Legal documents are where translation errors cost the most: a misread clause, a wrong party name or a shifted obligation can change what a contract means. Law firms, notaries, compliance teams and in-house counsel regularly need contracts, filings and certificates translated — often under deadline.

Why legal documents need a different approach

Three things make legal translation unforgiving. First, terminology: legal terms are jurisdiction-specific — "consideration" in English contract law has no exact equivalent in civil-law systems, and each language has formulaic equivalents that must be used consistently. Second, fidelity: a legal translation must track the source sentence by sentence; fluent paraphrasing is a defect, not a feature. Third, completeness: nothing may be omitted, including footnotes, annexes and signature blocks.

This is why DataCompiler flags uncertain passages for human review instead of silently guessing — the review layer exists precisely for the terms where a wrong word changes the meaning.

Document types commonly translated

  • Commercial contracts, NDAs and terms of service
  • Court filings, judgments and powers of attorney
  • Company documents: articles of association, board resolutions, registry extracts
  • Due-diligence materials and compliance certificates
  • Litigation documents and evidence bundles

Languages covered

DataCompiler translates between English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Polish, Turkish and Chinese (Simplified) — covering most intra-European legal work and EU trade documentation. Legal documents are priced at a difficulty-adjusted rate reflecting the review they need; you see the exact price and delivery date before ordering.

Important caveat

DataCompiler produces a faithful, reviewed translation — it is not a substitute for a sworn/certified translation where one is legally required (court submissions, civil registry documents in some jurisdictions). For those, use the output as a working draft and have it certified by an authorised translator.
