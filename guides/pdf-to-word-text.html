How to convert a PDF to Word or text

Reading time: 4 min

"Convert PDF to Word" is one of the most searched document tasks — but the right method depends entirely on what kind of PDF you have. A born-digital PDF (created from Word, InDesign, LaTeX) already contains real text and converts cleanly. A scanned PDF is a stack of images and needs OCR first. This guide covers both.

Step 1: identify your PDF type

Open the PDF and try to select the text. If you can highlight words, it's native — conversion will be easy. If selection fails or produces garbage, it's a scan: the page is an image and OCR is required before any text extraction.

Native PDFs: direct conversion

For native PDFs, most tools work: Adobe Acrobat, Word itself (File → Open), free converters, or command-line tools like pdftotext. The output preserves text content but rarely preserves perfect layout — columns, tables and text boxes get reorganized. If you need the look preserved, converting page-by-page to images or keeping the PDF is often better than forcing editable text.

Scanned PDFs: OCR first

For scans, the workflow is OCR → text → editable format. Quality depends on scan resolution and contrast. Tables, handwriting, stamps and multi-column layouts are the usual failure points — always proofread OCR output before relying on it.

PDF to plain text vs PDF to Word

  • Plain text (.txt) — fastest, loses all formatting. Best for search, translation pipelines, data extraction.
  • Word (.docx) — keeps headings, lists, basic formatting. Best when the content will be edited.
  • Keep as PDF — often the right answer for documents that will be shared unchanged; PDFs are compact and universal.
